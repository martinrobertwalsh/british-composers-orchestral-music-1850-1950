Mary Anderson Lucas (1882–1952)

“Juggling motherhood and music”

The Circus, suite for orchestra (1939)

Lucas was an English composer and pianist whose most active composing years came after marriage and motherhood. Born Mary Anderson Juler in London on 24 May 1882, she studied piano in Dresden and at the Royal Academy of Music. She later studied composition with Herbert Howells and R. O. Morris at the Royal College of Music.

Her marriage to motor-car designer and inventor Ralph Lucas in 1903 interrupted her musical career. Returning to composition during the 1920s, she gained performances of her orchestral music in Bournemouth and subsequently attracted attention through chamber concerts and BBC broadcasts. Her Clarinet Sonata of 1938 was written for her niece, the distinguished clarinettist Pauline Juler.

Among her orchestral compositions, The Circus provides a particularly appealing introduction. A newspaper review identified as appearing in the Kentish Independent in March 1937 records her playing three pieces from the suite: The Clown, The Tightrope Walker and The Trapeze Act. The critic praised their effectiveness, describing impressionistic musical pictures capturing the atmosphere of the circus ring.

Her circus-themed ballet Sawdust was performed in London and Wolverhampton in 1941 by the Ballet Guild, directed by conductor and composer Leighton Lucas, who was unrelated to her. Accounts describe the ballet as subsequently orchestrated as the Circus Suite. Its development nevertheless needs clarification: the British Library provisionally dates the orchestral score [1939], while the earlier newspaper review documents piano pieces under the Circus title. The music therefore appears to have developed through several forms.

Dedicated to Leighton Lucas, the orchestral suite received a prominent performance at the Royal Albert Hall on 4 July 1942, conducted by Sir Henry Wood. This marked Mary Lucas’s Proms debut at sixty.

Her music continued to receive attention during the war. A newspaper report reproduced on WikiTree, dated 31 May 1945, discusses an experimental rehearsal of her Ballet Preludes by the London Symphony Orchestra at the Guildhall School of Music. The critic recognised a Sibelian influence, particularly in its slower movements.
